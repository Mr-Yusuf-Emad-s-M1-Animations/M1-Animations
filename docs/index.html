  • Vertical projection

    A stone is thrown straight up from A. If A is above the ground, it falls past A down to the ground. The velocity-time graph is a straight line of gradient -g.

Chapter 2: Statics 3 scenes

  • Particle on two strings

    A particle hangs in equilibrium from two strings, or from a string and a force. Watch the pulls grow to balance the weight.

    2.12.2

  • On the point of sliding

    The force P grows steadily. While P is small the particle is about to slide DOWN and friction acts up the slope; when P is large it is about to slide UP and friction flips to act down the slope. Use the scrub bar as a slider for P.

    2.52.82.92.10

  • Particle on a rough surface with an angled force

    Make a force on a particle bigger and watch the friction change until the particle is about to slide. The force can be at any angle to the surface.

    2.42.52.62.72.82.9

Chapter 3: Dynamics 8 scenes

  • Rough inclined plane

    A block slides down a rough slope. Watch the forces W, N and F.

    2.123.3

  • Projected up a rough slope

    A particle is projected up a rough slope. Friction acts down the slope while it moves up, then flips to act up the slope when it slides back, so the two accelerations are different.

    3.113.12

  • Car and trailer

    Connected particles on a road. A rod can pull (tension) or push (thrust). A string can only pull: ask it to push and it goes slack, and the trailer is on its own.

    3.73.14

  • Person in a lift

    The lift speeds up, moves at constant speed, then slows to rest. The force from the floor R = m(g + a), with a positive upwards: R is biggest when the acceleration points up and smallest when it points down.

    3.103.13

  • Two particles over a pulley

    A is released from height h. When A lands, the string goes slack and B keeps rising, then falls back down. Both bounce on the ground.

    3.153.163.173.21

  • Table and pulley

    A hanging particle pulls a particle along a rough table. When it lands, the string goes slack and the hanging particle bounces.

    3.153.16

  • Pulley and rough slope

    A particle on a rough slope is joined over a pulley to a hanging particle. If the hanging particle descends, it can land on the ground and bounce.

    3.153.173.21

  • Pulley: an extra mass falls away

    A, carrying an extra mass, pulls B up. After A falls a distance d the extra mass is caught by a ring and left behind, so the acceleration changes - often A then slows down.

    3.19

Chapter 4: Momentum 4 scenes

  • Direct collision

    Two particles collide on a smooth line. Right is the positive direction.

    4.14.24.34.44.54.64.74.8

  • String jerks taut

    P moves away from Q along a smooth table. The string between them is slack until P has moved the slack distance; then it jerks tight and both move on together with the same speed.

    4.10

  • Ball bounces off a wall

    A ball moves along a smooth floor, hits a wall at right angles and rebounds. The velocity changes sign, so the change in momentum is m(u + v), not m(u − v).

    4.94.114.13

  • Sinking into soft ground

    An object falls freely, hits soft ground and is brought to rest over a short distance by a constant resistance R. In the ground: weight down, R up, so R − mg = m × deceleration.

    4.12
